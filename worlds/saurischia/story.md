# Saurischia

East of Hemlow, past the top of Raven Scar, the old block of country goes on.
The village calls its edge the Grimwold and keeps away from it. What lies
behind is a land where the great animals never died out, and where people
came, a long time ago, and stayed to live beside them.

## The land

Saurischia is the high ground the Scar is the edge of: an old upland of hard
grey rock that was never worn down, standing a hundred metres and more over
the sea. It runs east and north past every horizon. Along its west side, where
it meets Hemlow, it is covered in the Grimwold, monkey-puzzles over tree ferns
and cycads in a dim green light, and the Grimwold thins east into the
Fernwood, the great forest that fills the middle of the country.

In the east the upland steps up again. A second, younger fault crosses the
land from north-east to south, the Steps, a broken cliff forty to fifty metres
high with the high country behind it. The Saur, the one big river, comes in
off that high country from the east and goes over the Steps in a single white
drop, the Veil. At the foot of the Veil it has scoured out a broad round lake,
Cascade Mere, and in the middle of the mere there is an island of rock the
water never moved.

The mere spills two ways. To the west a stream leaves it through the Fernwood,
slows into a marsh of horsetails, runs on through the Grimwold and goes over
Raven Scar into Hemlow, where they call it the Wend. To the south a second
stream, the Lessa, leaves past the lower farms and runs off the map toward the
coast.

In the south-west stands Cinderhorn, a young volcano. Its cone rises out of
the forest close to the south edge, and its last eruption, a little over three
hundred years ago, sent lava south down its flank and off past the edge of the
map, and laid ash over the forest north of it. That lava is still not cold:
its crust is black and broken, and in the cracks and in the channels that
still run at the bottom of the flow it glows at night. Ash from the same
eruption buried the forest's edge north-east of the cone, and the ash is where
the most famous bones in the country lie.

In the north the land opens. North of the Fernwood is a park of grass and
scattered monkey-puzzles, the Parkland, where the long-necked herds browse,
and north and east of that, on the high country over the Steps, the open
grass of Thunderfield, where the horned and duck-billed herds graze and the
big sauropods come to nest.

## The animals

Everything that walks here walks among people. The long-necks, brachiosaurs
and diplodocus, wander the Parkland and come down to the mere to drink; the
horned triceratops and the crested parasaurolophus graze Thunderfield in
herds, and stegosaurs and ankylosaurs keep to the forest edges. Flocks of
ostrich-like gallimimus race over the grass. Compsognathus pick round every
farmyard and through the streets of the city, and archaeopteryx flutter in the
tree ferns. Pteranodons wheel over the mere and nest on the face of the Steps
beside the Veil, and the great quetzalcoatlus stalks the grass on its folded
wings.

The meat-eaters keep to the deep Fernwood and the ash country under
Cinderhorn: the raptors in packs, and now and then a tyrannosaur. People do
not go into the deep wood alone.

## The people

They came up the Saur from the east, nobody remembers when, and found the mere
and the island. On the island they built Cascadelle, a city of the water:
stone palaces standing straight out of the canals with arcades along their
fronts, tall houses with grey mansard roofs along the boulevards on the high
middle of the island, and a domed rotunda in the middle where the canals
meet. Every gate and arcade on the main ways is built high enough for a
long-neck to walk through, because they do. Bridges cross the canals and
the ring of the mere to the shore, and a road climbs the Steps beside the
Veil to a terrace at the top where you can stand in the spray and look
down on the whole city.

The city eats from two belts of farms: the upper farms north of the mere,
between the Fernwood and the Parkland, and the lower farms south of it along
the Lessa, with orchards, vines and fields of wheat and barley, and hedges
the animals mostly respect.

On the north road, where it comes out of the Fernwood into the Parkland,
stands Ferncross, a timber town of travellers and foresters. Its houses are
built up off the ground on posts, out of the way of anything walking through
at night. From Ferncross the north road goes west along the top of the
country into Hemlow's rising ground and down to the north road there, and a
second road goes west from Cascadelle through the Fernwood to the top of
Hemlow's switchback. Nobody from Hemlow uses either.

## Bones

The country is full of bones, and its people care for the great ones.

- **The Sleeper**, in a fern glade in the Fernwood north-west of the mere: the
  skeleton of a long-neck far larger than any alive now, lying on its side
  with its neck curled round, its ribs standing out of the ferns.
- **Ashjaw**, a tyrannosaur in the ash at the toe of the lava, caught by the
  eruption three hundred years ago and uncovered by the rain since: the
  skull, the spine and the hips standing out of the grey ground.
- **Hornbone Gully** on the Parkland: a gully cut through a bed of
  triceratops bones, a whole herd that died in a flood.
- **The Arch**, in a glade south-west of the mere: the neck of a diplodocus
  arching out of a stream bank and back into it.

## The places

- **The Grimwold**, along the west edge, carrying on from Hemlow's.
- **The Fernwood**: high forest of monkey-puzzles and ginkgos on the higher
  ground, tree-fern brakes in the hollows, the oldest monkey-puzzles in the
  Titan Stand, a dawn redwood swamp in the wet ground west of the Lessa, and
  fern glades.
- **The horsetail marsh** where the Wend slows.
- **Cascadelle**, the mere, and the Veil.
- **Spray Meadow** at the foot of the Steps under the Veil.
- **The upper and lower farms.**
- **Ferncross.**
- **The Parkland** and **Thunderfield**, with **the Nestings**.
- **Cinderhorn**, its crater, the young lava and the hot springs on its west
  flank.

## Unexplained

- Why the animals lived on here and nowhere else. Strangeness is strongest in
  the Grimwold along the west edge, the edge of the old country, and fades
  east toward the city.
- The Sleeper is twice the length of the largest long-neck alive. Nobody has
  seen anything like it walking.
