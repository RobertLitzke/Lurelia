# Jadewater

A shallow green sea with a forest of stone standing in it. Limestone towers,
flat on top and sheer on every side, rise thirty to a hundred and twenty metres
out of water you could wade across in places. In the north-west they crowd
together into a single block, the Old Wall, cut through by gorges so narrow and
deep the sun reaches their floors for an hour a day. Between the Wall and the
sea is a strip of jungle, and in the jungle the ground has fallen in here and
there into round green pools.

## The stone

The rock is a pale blue-grey limestone, layered, laid down on the floor of a
warm sea long ago and lifted until it stood a little above the water. Rain did
the rest. Limestone dissolves in rain that has passed through soil, and over a
very long time the rain cut the plateau along its joints into blocks, the
blocks into towers, and the towers into thinner towers. The Old Wall is the
part of the plateau that has not finished being cut: its gorges are the joints
the water found first. Out in the lagoon the work is further along, and only
the towers are left, standing in the shallows on a floor of white lime sand.

Water went down through the rock as well as round it. Every tower and the Wall
itself are threaded with passages, level ones where the water table stood for a
long time, and chimneys where it ran down to them. Where a cave roof fell in,
the ground above it dropped into a sinkhole, and the sea came up into it from
below. These are the cenotes. The biggest of them, the Lantern Well, opened in
the middle of the Old Wall and is eighty metres deep from the rim to the water.
The jungle cenotes are smaller, each in a low hump of limestone that stands a
few metres over the trees, hollow underneath.

## The light

The stone is still being made. The cave ceilings of Jadewater, and the
overhangs of the cenotes, are covered by a living film that gives off a cold
cyan light. It feeds on the lime in the dripping water and lays the lime down
again as stone, a fraction of a millimetre a year. Where it is thick the
ceilings glow bright enough to walk by. Nobody who lived here knew whether the
film came with the stone or the stone came with the film.

The film is strongest in the Lantern Well and in the passages nearest it, and it fades away from there: the lagoon towers have dark caves, except the
seven towers of the Choir, whose passages glow like the Well's. In the
cenotes it has changed what grows. The water lilies there have pads three and
four metres across, thick and stiff enough to walk on, and they sit in the
light of the overhangs. The film also feeds the rays.

## The rays

Kite rays are the size of a door, jade-backed, spotted with the same cyan
light, and they fly. They rise out of the cenotes on the warm, damp air, ride
the updrafts on the sunny faces of the towers, and drift down into the gorges
at dusk. Nobody has seen one take food. The monks thought they grazed the light
off the overhangs. Out over the open lagoon there are ordinary mantas, which
stay in the water.

## The Order of the Lantern

About four hundred years ago a religious order came here by sea. They called
the glow in the caves the lantern and built a temple in the jungle south of
the Old Wall, among the banyans, with the Wall standing over the trees behind
it: a hall, a small pagoda, a bell house, stone lanterns that the film has
grown into and still glow, rows of little stone figures, and an avenue of
torii gates walking up from the south to the hall. They lived on the lowland, fished the
lagoon and cut bamboo in the gorges.

The monks did not stay below. They widened the passages through the towers,
cut stairs and chimneys, and hung bridges between the tops: rope and plank,
lacquered coral red, the same red as the gates. The Red Way crosses the gorges
of the Old Wall at the height of its top, and a second line of bridges runs
out over the lagoon on towers whose tops the monks levelled to one height so
the bridges would hang true. On the Wall's top and the tower tops the pines are
small and twisted by the wind, and the monks left them alone.

The order left a hundred and twenty years ago. There is no account of why. The
temple is going to moss, the roofs are opening, vines are on everything and
the banyans are in the walls. The bridges are still red. The lacquer was mixed,
the temple's few surviving notes say, with oil from the lantern film, and it
has not faded.

## The Choir

Out in the lagoon east of the temple, seven towers stand in a ring around a
blue hole, a round shaft in the sea floor going down into the dark. The towers
are the same height to within a hand's width and the same distance apart. The
rain does not cut towers like that. The monks did not build them: the order's
first notes describe the ring as it is now. The water in the blue hole is cold,
the lilies will not grow there, and the rays circle over it in the evening and
do not settle.

The Choir, the cave light that lays down stone, and rays that swim in the air
are the three things left unexplained, and they are the same question: what
the film is, and whether the towers are something it is building.

## The living things

The gorges are full of golden bamboo, thin culms close enough together that
you push between them, and the bamboo forest on the wetter ground east of the
Wall has giant bamboo twenty metres high among ferns and small broadleaf
trees. Pandas live in the bamboo, a few dozen of them, and do not care about
people. Serow, small dark mountain goats, live on the tower ledges and the top
of the Wall. In the jungle there are boar and deer and banyans, which start
as seeds in a crack, send roots down the rock and in the end hold the stone
up, or pull it down: the biggest grow straight out of the cliffs. Herons and
cormorants fish the lagoon, turtles come up on the white beaches, and an eagle
works the updrafts over the towers.

## What happened, in order

1. A limestone plateau lifted a little above a warm shallow sea.
2. Rain cut it along its joints into the Old Wall and its gorges, and
   further out into free-standing towers in a lagoon of lime sand.
3. Caves formed; roofs fell; the cenotes opened, the Lantern Well biggest.
4. The ring of seven towers, the Choir, around the blue hole (unexplained).
5. The film spreads out from the Lantern Well; giant lilies, kite rays.
6. A small river runs out of the Wall's main gorge to the lagoon.
7. About 400 years ago the Order of the Lantern builds the temple, the torii
   avenue, the passages and the Red Way.
8. About 120 years ago the order leaves. Moss, vines and banyans since.
