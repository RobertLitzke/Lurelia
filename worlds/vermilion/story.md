# Vermilion Mesa

A figure-eight in red rock country: a broad sweep along a canyon floor, switchbacks up a ravine of aspens, a run through the pines on top of the mesa, a plunge through the boulders and a long tunnel through the mesa's foot.

The track is built from tools/course/maps/vermilion.track by tools/course/track_file.py; the world round it is this folder's chronicle.

## The country

This is the same rock as Redrock's, a little further up the same river system, where the beds lie flat and the water has had less time. Lowest is the banded purple and grey mudstone, which makes the floors and the soft slopes at the foot of every wall. Over it is the dark red Wingate sandstone, which breaks in sheer faces, then a few metres of purple Kayenta ledges, then the salmon Navajo sandstone, cream at the very top. A thin pale band runs round every cliff high enough to reach it, at the same height to within a hand. Redrock's miners called it the high-water mark, and nobody here has a better name for it.

A creek once wound through a bench of this rock and cut down through it in a long loop. What was left inside the loop is Vermilion Mesa, about four hundred metres across and thirty-two metres high, its walls sheer, flat on top under its Navajo cap. The outside of the loop is the Vermilion Cliffs, the wall of the old bench, which rings the basin on the west, south and east; a lower rim closes it to the north. The creek is long gone. Flash floods off the bench still cut short side canyons back into the cliffs, and the floor of the loop is a dry, flat canyon of red sand, sagebrush and juniper.

On the west the mesa runs out in a broad spur. Its end has broken away in big blocks of caprock, which lie in a fan down the slope below it as the Rockfall. North of the spur the canyon opens into the arch valley, where the pale sandstone stands in fins and the weather has worn windows and arches through them: Vermilion Arch standing free in the middle, Broad Arch and Ribbon Arch on the north side, the Spectacles, and a dome with a pothole worn through to the ground. Hoodoos stand in two groups, the Chimneys under the Vermilion Cliffs south of the canyon road and the Goblins at the foot of the Rockfall.

## Aspen Draw

Water that sinks through the mesa top comes out again at a spring under the north arm of the mesa's east face. Its creek has cut back a draw into that face, Aspen Draw, and keeps it damp enough for grass and a grove of aspen. The creek runs down the draw past the switchbacks, under the road twice, and ends in a pool at the draw's mouth, where a few cottonwoods have found it. A butte on the draw's east side, the Sentry, was cut off from the mesa when the creek ran further east.

## The pines

The mesa top holds enough soil for a ponderosa forest: tall orange-barked pines with open ground between, needle duff, gambel oak, a few snags and fallen trunks. It is the only forest for miles, and from the canyon floor it shows as a dark line along the top of the red walls.

## The course

The road was graded for racing. It starts on the canyon floor and runs east along the inside of the loop, close under the mesa's south face, then climbs Aspen Draw in three legs and two hairpins and crosses the mesa top west through the pines. Over the spur it passes directly above its own tunnel, then eases off the spur's broken end and down the Rockfall fan, loops round the arch valley, and goes back south into the tunnel.

The tunnel was bored through the foot of the spur, two hundred metres from the north face to the south, and lit along its roof. Its portals are lined in laid red stone where they stand out of the cliffs; inside, the bore shows the beds it was cut through, purple mudstone below and red Wingate above, over a floor of packed red clay. It comes out on the canyon floor a few hundred metres short of the start.
